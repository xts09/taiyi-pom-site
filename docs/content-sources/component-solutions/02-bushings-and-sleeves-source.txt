3. Component Scope
Plastic bushings and sleeves are cylindrical or flanged components used to support, locate, guide, or separate shafts, pins, pivots, and other moving members. Depending on the mechanism, motion may be rotational, oscillating, or linear, and the component may operate dry, initially lubricated, or continuously exposed to a fluid. ISO plain-bearing terminology treats bearing design, material behavior, friction, and wear as distinct engineering subjects. For molded polymer bushings, performance is strongly affected by bore geometry, shaft condition, running clearance, load distribution, temperature, debris, and assembly method—not simply by the base polymer. [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/86318.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

4. Engineering Priorities
4.1 Bearing Pressure, Sliding Speed, and Heat Generation
Why it matters:
Pressure and sliding velocity are fundamental screening variables for polymer plain bearings because friction converts mechanical energy into heat. However, a published PV limit should not be treated as a universal material constant. Heat dissipation, clearance, shaft material, lubrication, geometry, and duty cycle can substantially change real performance, and different laboratory methods may produce different rankings. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
Information the customer should provide:
Radial or side load
Shaft diameter and loaded bearing length
Rotational or sliding speed
Continuous versus intermittent duty
Possible validation method:
ISO 7148-2-based tribological screening followed by production-intent bushing testing at representative load, speed, and temperature. [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/88052.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
Risks if ignored:
Thermal softening, accelerated wear, seizure, excessive clearance growth, or misleading material comparisons.
4.2 Motion Type and Duty Cycle
Why it matters:
Continuous rotation, short-angle oscillation, reciprocating linear motion, and occasional adjustment do not create the same wear pattern. Oscillatory joints may repeatedly load the same surface zone, while continuous rotation distributes sliding differently. Startup frequency, dwell periods, reversing motion, and shock loads also affect frictional heating and local contact conditions.
Information the customer should provide:
Rotary, oscillating, or linear motion
Stroke or oscillation angle
Cycle frequency
Expected operating life
Possible validation method:
Component test reproducing the real motion profile and duty cycle.
Risks if ignored:
A material may perform well in continuous laboratory rotation but wear rapidly in a short-stroke or reversing application.
4.3 Shaft Material, Hardness, and Surface Condition
Why it matters:
The shaft is part of the bearing material system. POM tribology studies show that steel counterface topography, pressure, velocity, and temperature can change friction and wear behavior. Engineering guidance for polymer bearings likewise identifies shaft hardness and surface finish as major design variables. [科学直通车](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0043164882902265?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
Information the customer should provide:
Shaft or pin material
Surface treatment or coating
Surface finish
Shaft wear or corrosion condition
Possible validation method:
Tribological comparison using production-intent shaft samples, followed by surface and wear-track inspection.
Risks if ignored:
Unexpected bushing wear, shaft scoring, transfer-film instability, noise, or abrasive wear.
4.4 Running Clearance and Dimensional Change
Why it matters:
The functional clearance exists between the installed bushing and shaft, not on the bushing drawing alone. Plastic bearing dimensions can change through molding shrinkage, thermal expansion, environmental exposure, and press-fit deformation. Engineering guidance specifically identifies bore accuracy and running clearance as critical to polymer bearing performance. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
Information the customer should provide:
Shaft tolerance
Housing bore tolerance
Required installed clearance
Operating temperature range
Possible validation method:
Measure the bushing before installation, after press fitting, and after thermal/environmental conditioning.
Risks if ignored:
Binding, high startup torque, excessive free play, shaft misalignment, or unstable clearance over service life.
4.5 Housing Fit and Retention
Why it matters:
A molded sleeve frequently relies on interference with the housing to remain stationary. That interference also compresses the polymer and changes the finished bore. Too little retention can allow the bushing to rotate or migrate; excessive interference can reduce running clearance, distort the bore, or generate long-term stress and creep.
Information the customer should provide:
Housing material
Housing bore and tolerance
Installation method
Axial and rotational retention requirements
Possible validation method:
Installed-bore measurement, insertion/push-out testing, thermal cycling, and long-duration retention testing.
Risks if ignored:
Bushing walk-out, housing spin, bore closure, cracking, or reduced shaft clearance.
4.6 Lubrication, Dust, and Wear Debris
Why it matters:
Dry-running capability does not mean that contamination is irrelevant. Published polymer-bearing guidance notes that dirt and metallic wear debris can enter the interface and increase abrasive wear, while grooves or interruptions may help remove debris from the sliding surface. Lubricant can also interact with contamination and must be considered as part of the tribological system. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
Information the customer should provide:
Dry, initially lubricated, or continuously lubricated
Lubricant type
Dust, textile fiber, sand, or process debris
Sealing or shielding arrangement
Possible validation method:
Clean and contaminated endurance tests using the intended lubricant and shaft.
Risks if ignored:
Abrasive scoring, accelerated wear, lubricant contamination, or inconsistent field life.
4.7 Alignment and Edge Loading
Why it matters:
A nominal bearing-pressure calculation assumes useful contact over the intended bearing length. Housing distortion, shaft misalignment, bore taper, ovality, or insufficient concentricity can concentrate the load at one edge. Molded geometry also matters: nonuniform shrinkage can prevent a nominally cylindrical bushing from carrying load uniformly. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
Information the customer should provide:
Shaft support arrangement
Housing geometry
Bore concentricity requirement
Observed wear pattern
Possible validation method:
Roundness/concentricity measurement, contact-pattern inspection, and loaded assembly testing.
Risks if ignored:
One-sided wear, local overheating, flange damage, shaft scoring, and premature clearance growth.

6. Candidate Material Directions
6.1 Unfilled POM
When it may be considered:
Precision bushings and sleeves with moderate mechanical loading where dimensional stability, good sliding behavior, moldability, low moisture sensitivity, and a useful balance of stiffness and toughness are required.
Potential advantages:
POM is widely used in sliding engineering components and combines stiffness, dimensional stability, relatively low water uptake, and favorable sliding-friction characteristics. [巴斯夫塑料与橡胶](https://plastics-rubber.basf.com/northamerica/en/performance_polymers/products/ultraform?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
Important tradeoffs:
Unfilled POM does not eliminate the influence of shaft roughness, temperature, pressure, velocity, contamination, or clearance.
Required project validation:
Installed dimensions, friction, wear rate, shaft condition, temperature rise, and service-life testing.
Situations where it may not be appropriate:
High sustained temperature, severe abrasive contamination, excessive contact pressure, or applications requiring a different thermal or structural performance envelope.
6.2 Wear-Resistant or Low-Friction POM
When it may be considered:
When measurable wear, startup friction, stick-slip, noise, or thermal generation limits the performance of a standard POM direction.
Potential advantages:
Tribological modification may reduce friction or wear in a defined shaft/material combination.
Important tradeoffs:
Lower friction does not automatically mean lower wear. Historical bearing guidance explicitly warns that filled formulations can raise apparent PV capability through lower friction without necessarily providing better wear behavior. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
Required project validation:
Use the production shaft, lubricant, pressure, speed, temperature, and duty cycle.
Situations where it may not be appropriate:
When the root cause is insufficient clearance, misalignment, contaminated lubrication, poor shaft finish, or housing-fit error.
6.3 Reinforced POM
When it may be considered:
Where higher stiffness, reduced deformation, or improved resistance to housing or external mechanical loading is more important than minimizing tribological complexity.
Potential advantages:
Reinforcement can improve stiffness and reduce structural deformation.
Important tradeoffs:
Glass-fiber orientation makes shrinkage anisotropic and can increase warpage sensitivity. A harder reinforced sliding surface may also interact differently with the shaft, so counterface wear needs explicit evaluation. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/delrin-acetal-homopolymer-thermoplastic-resin-molding-guide/)
Required project validation:
Installed bore geometry, shaft wear, friction, creep, molding variation, and fiber-orientation effects.
Situations where it may not be appropriate:
Very small precision bushings, soft counterfaces, or applications where isotropic shrinkage and smooth sliding behavior dominate.
6.4 Conductive or Antistatic Compounds
When it may be considered:
Where the bushing participates in a mechanism with a defined electrostatic-discharge or charge-dissipation requirement, such as selected electronics-handling or automation systems.
Potential advantages:
A modified compound may provide electrical dissipation while preserving injection-molded component integration.
Important tradeoffs:
Conductive fillers can alter friction, wear, stiffness, shrinkage, surface quality, and mechanical fatigue.
Required project validation:
Electrical resistance together with wear, dimensional, shaft, and installed-component testing.
Situations where it may not be appropriate:
Ordinary mechanical bushings without a defined electrical requirement.
6.5 PA6, PA66, or PPA
When it may be considered:
PA6 or PA66 may be worth evaluating where a different balance of toughness, abrasive-wear behavior, or mechanical response is needed. PPA may be considered where elevated temperature and humidity create a performance requirement beyond the practical range of the POM options being evaluated.
Potential advantages:
Different polyamide families can provide different toughness and thermal-property combinations; PPA can provide better elevated-temperature property retention and lower moisture uptake than standard PA6 or PA66 families. [巴斯夫塑料与橡胶](https://plastics-rubber.basf.com/northamerica/en/performance_polymers/industries/pp_electronics_and_electric/products/ultramid_advancedt2000?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
Important tradeoffs:
Moisture absorption in PA6 and PA66 can produce dimensional change and alter mechanical behavior, which is particularly relevant to installed bearing clearance. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/app.27057?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
Required project validation:
Conditioned dimensions, hot/wet properties, shaft compatibility, wear, and molding process.
Situations where it may not be appropriate:
Tight-clearance bushings where moisture-driven dimensional control is more problematic than the benefit gained from changing polymer family.
7. Design, Tooling, and Processing Considerations
7.1 Control Wall Thickness and Circular Symmetry
A sleeve depends on a round, concentric bore. Large wall-thickness changes, external ribs, asymmetric flanges, or adjoining structures can cool and shrink differently around the circumference. Semi-crystalline POM molding guidance identifies wall-thickness variation and nonuniform mold temperature as potential causes of distortion. Keep the bearing region as geometrically balanced as practical. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/delrin-acetal-homopolymer-thermoplastic-resin-molding-guide/)
7.2 Treat the Core Pin as a Precision Feature
The bearing ID is often formed directly by a core pin. Long or insufficiently supported cores can deflect under melt pressure and create taper or eccentricity. General engineering-plastic design guidance specifically notes that unsupported core pins forming deep holes can shift under melt flow. For a precision sleeve, core stiffness and support deserve the same attention as the nominal cavity diameter. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
7.3 Balance Gate Location Around the Bore
An asymmetric fill pattern can push the core, create nonuniform packing, and establish different shrinkage around the sleeve. Gate strategy should therefore be reviewed for bore concentricity and roundness, not just filling convenience. Where reinforced compounds are considered, flow direction becomes even more important because fiber orientation affects directional shrinkage. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/delrin-acetal-homopolymer-thermoplastic-resin-molding-guide/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
7.4 Specify the Installed Bore, Not Only the Molded Bore
A bushing intended for interference installation can have a different ID after pressing into the housing. Measuring loose parts alone can therefore miss the functional condition. Dimensional approval should include the housing, assembly method, temperature conditioning, and production-intent shaft. ISO 12301 specifically includes solid polymer bushes within its scope for geometric and material quality control. [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/36511.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
7.5 Design the Press Fit as an Assembly System
More interference is not automatically safer. Excessive interference can distort the bore or impose unnecessary hoop stress, while insufficient interference can permit movement. Entry chamfers, housing material, housing stiffness, sleeve wall thickness, assembly speed, and long-term temperature should all be considered before setting the dimensional stack.
7.6 Consider Debris Relief Where the Mechanism Allows It
A completely uninterrupted bearing surface is not always optimal. Polymer-bearing design guidance describes grooves or openings as a means of allowing wear debris to leave the primary rubbing interface. This should be evaluated against load-bearing area, lubricant retention, sealing, and molding feasibility rather than applied mechanically to every design. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
7.7 Control Packing, Cooling, and Cavity-to-Cavity Variation
POM is semi-crystalline, so packing and cooling conditions affect shrinkage and final dimensions. Reinforced grades add orientation-dependent shrinkage. For high-volume bushings, inspect bore diameter, OD, roundness, concentricity, flange geometry, and installed clearance by cavity rather than assuming all cavities behave identically. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/delrin-acetal-homopolymer-thermoplastic-resin-molding-guide/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

9. Frequently Asked Questions
1. Is POM a good material for plastic bushings?
POM is a credible candidate for many molded bushings because it combines dimensional stability, stiffness, relatively low moisture uptake, and useful sliding-friction behavior. It should not be approved from those family-level properties alone. The shaft material and finish, pressure, speed, motion type, installed clearance, temperature, lubricant, contamination, and expected life all influence whether unfilled or modified POM is technically suitable. [巴斯夫塑料与橡胶](https://plastics-rubber.basf.com/northamerica/en/performance_polymers/products/ultraform?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
2. When should I use a low-friction or wear-resistant POM?
Consider it when friction, wear, noise, or heat generation has been shown to be a limiting mechanism. Do not specify a tribological modifier simply because the component is called a bushing. Low friction and low wear are not synonymous, and compound ranking can change with shaft material, surface roughness, load, velocity, lubrication, and temperature. Representative paired-material testing is therefore more useful than comparing one datasheet number. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
3. How much clearance should a plastic bushing have?
There is no responsible universal clearance value for all molded POM bushings. The correct installed clearance depends on shaft diameter and tolerance, housing fit, bushing geometry, molding shrinkage, operating temperature, pressure, speed, environment, and required positional accuracy. Critically, interference installation can reduce the ID. Measure the bushing after installation and after relevant environmental conditioning rather than approving the free-state bore alone. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
4. Should the bushing run dry or with grease?
Both configurations may be viable, depending on the material and mechanism. Dry running eliminates lubricant management but places greater emphasis on the polymer/counterface tribosystem and heat generation. Lubrication can reduce friction or wear, but lubricant compatibility, migration, contamination, retention, and service environment must be considered. The actual lubricant should be included in validation rather than assuming a generic grease will produce the same result.
5. Does shaft surface finish really matter with a POM bushing?
Yes. POM-on-steel research and engineering bearing guidance both show that counterface condition can materially influence friction and wear. A rough, damaged, corroded, or inappropriate shaft can dominate the wear mechanism regardless of the polymer compound. Before changing resin, inspect the shaft surface and the wear track, and use production-intent shaft samples during tribological testing. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
6. POM or PA6/PA66: which is better for a bushing?
Neither is universally superior. POM is often attractive where dimensional control and relatively low moisture uptake are important. PA6 or PA66 may offer a useful alternative balance of toughness and tribological behavior, but water uptake can change their dimensions and properties. If the operating environment is hot and humid, PPA may also merit evaluation, although processing and economics need separate validation. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/app.27057?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
7. Why does a bushing fit correctly before assembly but bind afterward?
The most likely explanation is that the installed geometry differs from the loose-part geometry. Housing interference can compress the bushing, an out-of-round housing can distort it, or thermal and molding effects can shift the bore. Check ID, roundness, and shaft clearance after installation. If the bore closes excessively, the solution may involve tolerances, housing design, or assembly method rather than a different polymer.
8. What testing is needed before approving a new bushing material?
Use material-level tribology tests for screening, then validate molded components in the intended assembly. ISO 7148-2:2026 provides a framework for comparing friction and wear of polymer-based bearing materials under defined load, velocity, temperature, and lubrication conditions, but the standard itself reinforces the need for representative conditions. Track wear, friction or drive torque, temperature, clearance growth, shaft condition, and retention during endurance testing. [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/88052.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Plastic Bushings and Sleeves: Material Selection Guide
Sliding Component Solutions
Bushings and sleeves operate as part of a sliding system involving the polymer, shaft or pin, housing, clearance, load, speed, temperature, and lubrication condition. PLATFORM modified POM compounds can be evaluated where dimensional stability, sliding behavior, wear resistance, or stiffness need to be balanced.
Material selection should follow the actual operating conditions and production-intent component testing rather than a generic friction or wear value.
Bushings Are a System, Not an Isolated Plastic Part
Plastic bushings and sleeves support, locate, or guide shafts, pins, pivots, and other moving components. Typical designs include cylindrical bushings, flanged bushings, pivot sleeves, shaft guides, linkage bushings, and bearing sleeves.
Their motion may be rotational, oscillating, or linear. Some operate dry, others receive initial grease, and some remain exposed to lubricants, water, dust, or process contamination.
For this reason, selecting a bushing material requires more than checking tensile strength or a published coefficient of friction. Plain-bearing performance depends on the complete interface between the polymer, shaft, housing, environment, and motion system. [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/88052.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
Start with Load, Speed, and Motion
Bearing Pressure and Sliding Speed
Load and relative velocity influence frictional heating and wear. They are often combined into a PV value for preliminary bearing analysis, but PV should not be used as a universal pass/fail number.
Heat dissipation, shaft material, clearance, lubrication, geometry, and duty cycle also matter. Two bushings operating at similar nominal PV conditions can therefore have very different service lives. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
Provide the radial load, shaft diameter, bearing length, operating speed, and duty cycle when requesting a material evaluation.
Rotary, Oscillating, or Linear Motion
A continuously rotating shaft does not create the same wear pattern as a pivot moving through a small angle.
Oscillating joints repeatedly load a limited section of the bearing surface. Reciprocating sleeves experience reversing motion, while intermittently operated mechanisms may cool between cycles.
The validation test should reproduce the actual motion profile whenever practical.
The Shaft Is Part of the Material Selection
Counterface Material and Surface Condition
The polymer is only one side of the sliding interface. Steel, stainless steel, aluminum, coated shafts, and polymer pins can create different friction and wear behavior.
Research on POM sliding systems shows that counterface topography, load, velocity, and temperature influence tribological performance. Shaft hardness and finish should therefore be included in the application specification rather than treated as secondary information. [科学直通车](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0043164882902265?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
A rough or damaged shaft can make a good bearing compound perform poorly.
Do Not Ignore Shaft Wear
The bushing is not always the only sacrificial component. Reinforcement or tribological additives can change how the polymer interacts with the shaft.
When evaluating a new compound, inspect both surfaces after testing. A lower bushing wear rate is not useful if the mating shaft develops unacceptable scoring or dimensional loss.
Clearance Must Be Evaluated After Assembly
Free-State ID Is Not the Functional ID
A press-fit bushing normally changes dimensions when installed into its housing. The housing compresses the outer diameter, and some of that deformation can reduce the bore.
Thermal expansion, post-molding dimensional change, housing tolerance, and shaft tolerance further affect the final running clearance.
For this reason, specify and measure the installed bearing condition rather than approving the loose bushing only. Bore diameter, roundness, concentricity, and shaft clearance should be checked after assembly and, when relevant, after environmental conditioning. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
Too Little Clearance
Insufficient running clearance can increase startup torque, frictional heating, noise, or binding.
Before changing to a lower-friction polymer, verify whether the installed bore has closed more than expected or whether the shaft and housing are misaligned.
Too Much Clearance
Excessive clearance can create free play, vibration, poor positioning, impact loading, and uneven contact.
If clearance increases during service, determine whether the cause is abrasive wear, thermal deformation, creep, shaft damage, or housing movement before changing material.
Candidate POM Directions
Unfilled POM
Unfilled POM is a practical starting direction for many precision molded bushings because it offers a useful combination of stiffness, dimensional stability, relatively low moisture uptake, moldability, and sliding behavior. [巴斯夫塑料与橡胶](https://plastics-rubber.basf.com/northamerica/en/performance_polymers/products/ultraform?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
It still requires validation against the actual shaft, load, speed, temperature, lubrication, and service-life requirement.
Wear-Resistant or Low-Friction POM
A tribologically modified POM may be considered when wear, friction, noise, stick-slip, or temperature rise has been identified as a limiting factor.
Do not assume that the compound with the lowest published friction value will provide the longest bearing life. Friction and wear respond differently to the counterface and operating conditions, and published engineering guidance cautions against using low friction as a substitute for wear validation. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
The production shaft and lubricant should be included in comparative testing.
Reinforced POM
Reinforced POM may be considered where increased stiffness or reduced structural deformation is required.
The tradeoff is greater molding complexity. Fiber orientation introduces directional shrinkage and can affect bore geometry, warpage, and counterface interaction. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/delrin-acetal-homopolymer-thermoplastic-resin-molding-guide/)
For precision sliding bores, these effects need to be measured rather than assumed.
Conductive or Antistatic Materials
Electrical modification is relevant only when the mechanism has a defined ESD or conductivity requirement.
Conductive fillers can affect wear, stiffness, shrinkage, and friction, so electrical performance cannot be evaluated separately from mechanical bearing performance.
When PA6, PA66, or PPA May Be Relevant
POM should remain the primary candidate direction when the application favors dimensional stability and sliding performance, but another polymer family may sometimes deserve evaluation.
PA6 or PA66 may provide a useful alternative balance of toughness and wear behavior. However, moisture absorption can alter dimensions and mechanical behavior, which directly affects bearing clearance. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/app.27057?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
PPA may be considered when elevated temperature and humidity require greater retention of mechanical properties than standard aliphatic polyamides can provide. This is a separate engineering direction and requires its own processing, wear, dimensional, and cost validation. [巴斯夫塑料与橡胶](https://plastics-rubber.basf.com/northamerica/en/performance_polymers/industries/pp_electronics_and_electric/products/ultramid_advancedt2000?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
Design the Bushing for Injection Molding
Keep the Bearing Wall Balanced
A cylindrical bearing surface benefits from uniform shrinkage around the bore.
Large thickness transitions, asymmetric ribs, or uneven flange geometry can create different cooling conditions and distort the finished sleeve. Semi-crystalline POM is particularly sensitive to packing and cooling history when dimensional control is important. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/delrin-acetal-homopolymer-thermoplastic-resin-molding-guide/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
Protect Bore Concentricity
The core pin forming the ID is a precision tooling element.
Long or poorly supported core pins can deflect under melt pressure, producing an eccentric or tapered bore. Gate position and melt flow should therefore be reviewed together with the core design.
Validate the Press Fit
The housing fit must retain the bushing without closing the bore excessively.
Provide the housing material, bore tolerance, installation method, sleeve wall thickness, operating temperature, and required shaft clearance before finalizing the fit.
For critical applications, measure insertion force, installed ID, retention force, and dimensional change after thermal cycling.
Control Wear Debris
Dust, fibers, metal particles, and wear debris can turn the bearing interface into an abrasive system.
Where the geometry allows, relief grooves or other debris-management features may help remove particles from the primary rubbing surface. Their use should be balanced against load-bearing area and lubricant retention. [Delrin](https://www.delrin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Design-Principles-for-Engineering-Polymers.pdf)
Common Problems to Investigate Before Changing Material
Rapid bore wear: Check pressure, speed, shaft finish, alignment, temperature, contamination, and lubrication.
Binding after installation: Measure the installed bore and housing interference before specifying a lower-friction compound.
Uneven edge wear: Inspect shaft alignment, housing distortion, bore taper, and load distribution.
Bushing rotation in the housing: Review interference, creep, temperature, and axial or rotational retention features.
Scoring and abrasive debris: Identify whether particles come from the environment, shaft, bushing, or contaminated lubricant.
A resin change can be appropriate, but it should follow failure analysis rather than replace it.
